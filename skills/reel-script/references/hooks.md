# Hooks

The hook is the first spoken line and the cover text. A stranger decides in about two
seconds. All examples below are real lines from @build.with.navneet scripts (Nov 2026).

## Rules

1. **A name, a number or a known frame in the first line.** "Perplexity", "435 million
   dollars", "India's answer to OpenAI". Not "So I was reading this report…".
2. **Tension in sentence one.** A loss, a twist, a contrast, a claim that sounds wrong.
3. **Under 20 words, at most two sentences.** It also needs a 3–6 word version for the cover.
4. **True exactly as worded.** If a comment can say "that's not true", rewrite it.
5. **A frame the viewer knows beats a name they don't.** Most Indian viewers know
   OpenAI. Few know Sarvam. Lead with the frame, then give the name on line 2 or 3.
6. **The reviewer's own hook wins.** Use it word for word.

## Patterns that worked

| Pattern | Hook |
|---|---|
| Three beats, rising | Perplexity's first product died. Its second stopped growing. Its third just took it to a billion dollars a year. |
| Hidden identity | An actor's side quest just became one of the biggest wins in AI history. |
| Known frame + twist | India's answer to OpenAI and Anthropic just launched a new product. And most of the AI inside it is Chinese. |
| Contrast you can see on screen | Google's newest AI only runs in Google's cloud. This one runs on my desk with the Wi-Fi off. |
| Absurd personal claim (comedy) | I was also up for James Bond. Robert Pattinson took it from me. |

## Before → after (what the reviewer changed, and why)

**070 · Ashton Kutcher and Anthropic**
- v1: "In 2023, Ashton Kutcher invested in an AI company most people had never heard of.
  Today, that company went public. And on paper, his bet is worth about fifteen times more."
- Reviewer: put in the dollar figures and the IPO valuation; "fifteen times" is maths
  the viewer has to do.
- v3: "A Hollywood actor bet 435 million dollars on an AI company nobody was talking about.
  Today, that bet is worth seven and a half billion."
- Reviewer: "make the hook much better", then wrote his own.
- Final: "An actor's side quest just became one of the biggest wins in AI history."
  The dollar line moved to line 2. The name comes on line 3.
- Lesson: the hook sells the story. The numbers prove it on the next line.

**071 · Sarvam Token Factory**
- v1: "India picked Sarvam to build its own AI. Its new launch mostly runs Chinese AI."
- v2: "India's answer to ChatGPT just launched a new product…"
- Final (reviewer): "India's answer to OpenAI and Anthropic just launched a new product.
  And most of the AI inside it is Chinese."
- Lesson: a known frame beats an unknown name. Name the labs, not one app.

**072 · Perplexity at $1B a year**
- v1: "They called Perplexity 'just a wrapper'. It now makes a billion dollars a year."
- v2: "Perplexity just hit a billion dollars a year. But not from the product that made it famous."
- Final: "Perplexity's first product died. Its second stopped growing. Its third just took
  it to a billion dollars a year."
- Lesson: the reviewer asked for "a stronger hook". The winning line tells the whole arc
  in three beats, and the failures come first.

**073 · Pattinson cast as Bond**
- v1: "AI won't take your job. Robert Pattinson will." Funny, but no use to the viewer.
- Final: "I was also up for James Bond. Robert Pattinson took it from me." It opens a
  comedy "audition" that turns into a how-to (make your own scene from one selfie).
- Lesson: a meme hook still needs a body with a takeaway.

## Hooks to avoid

- Questions the viewer can answer "no" to: "Did you know…?", "Should you switch…?"
- "This changes everything", "This is huge", "You won't believe…"
- Setups that delay the point: "Here's the thing", "Let me tell you a story".
- Claims that are only partly true ("runs on any laptop", "nobody uses it").
